# Relight Studio AI 0.1.0 (public beta)

The first public build of Relight Studio AI, a Windows desktop app that changes the lighting of
a photo after it was taken. It estimates the scene's depth with an AI model, builds a 2.5D
version of the photo and lets you place and drag lights directly on the image.

This is a **beta**: relighting a single photo is an approximation (see *Known limitations*).
Feedback and bug reports are welcome in [Issues](https://github.com/cesaressau21/relight-studio-ai/issues).

## Download and install

1. Download **`RelightStudioAI-0.1.0-beta-setup.exe`** (about 2 MB) below.
2. Run it. No administrator rights are needed. If Windows SmartScreen warns about an unknown
   publisher (the setup is not code-signed yet), choose **More info → Run anyway**.
3. Setup copies the app and then downloads its components, with a window showing progress.
   Keep the computer online:

   | Your GPU | PyTorch build downloaded | Approx. download |
   |---|---|---|
   | NVIDIA GTX 16 / RTX 20 or newer (incl. RTX 50) with driver 580+ | CUDA 13.0 | ~3 GB |
   | Older NVIDIA GPU or older driver (not RTX 50) | CUDA 12.6 | ~3 GB |
   | No NVIDIA GPU | CPU | ~0.5 GB |

4. On first launch the app downloads its two AI models (~200 MB) from Hugging Face.

The Start menu gets **Relight Studio AI** and **Repair Relight Studio AI** (re-runs the download
after a connection drop or a driver update). Uninstall from Windows settings removes the app and
everything setup downloaded.

**Requirements:** Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). With an NVIDIA GPU the installed app takes about
4 GB, and setup needs about 7 GB free while it runs (the downloads are deleted afterwards).
Without a CUDA GPU everything works on the CPU, more slowly, and takes about 1 GB.

## What it does

- **Lights on the photo:** point, soft area, directional, ambient and rim lights. Drag them on
  the image and set depth (Z), intensity, radius, color or Kelvin temperature, softness,
  diffusion and falloff. There is no limit on the number of lights.
- **Light follows the shape:** surfaces facing a light brighten, the rest darken, and objects
  cast screen-space shadows onto what is behind them.
- **Subject and background:** AI matting separates the subject (down to hair). Each light can
  affect everything, only the subject or only the background.
- **Light patterns (gobos):** blinds, window panes, leaves, dots or your own image, projected
  over the estimated geometry.
- **Replace the background:** put the subject on another photo, and the app proposes lights that
  match the new background's direction and color.
- **Remove the original light's color:** take a teal or orange cast out of the subject, so new
  lights and backgrounds are not tinted by it. Optionally pick a neutral point.
- **Transparent PNG/WebP:** the transparency is kept and used as the exact subject outline.
- **Photo adjustments:** exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, saturation,
  white balance and gamma.
- **Ten presets:** Studio Portrait, Golden Hour, Cinematic Blue & Orange, Neon Cyberpunk, Soft
  Natural Light, Dramatic Side Light, Sunset, Moonlight, Venetian Blinds and Dappled Leaves.
- **Editing tools:** relight mask brush, depth correction brush, before/after split, undo/redo,
  and projects (`.rsproj`) that keep everything editable.
- **Export:** PNG, JPEG or WebP at full resolution with an sRGB profile, keeping transparency.
- **Runs locally:** after the first downloads, no internet connection is needed and no photo
  leaves your computer.

## Known limitations

- The depth is **relative** and estimated from one image: geometry, shadows and normals are
  plausible approximations, not a 3D reconstruction. Nothing hidden or outside the frame is known.
- Only **two layers** (subject and background). Parts of the subject at a similar distance to the
  background (a shoulder, a scarf) can be assigned to the wrong layer; adjust *Subject depth*.
- Removing the original light is partial: hard shadows of the original light can remain, and a
  uniformly colored light on a uniformly colored object cannot be told apart without picking a
  neutral point.
- Shadows are screen-space: objects are treated as infinitely thick and nothing off-screen casts
  shadows.
- Specular highlights fade on very dark materials (hair, eyebrows), because the depth map
  cannot resolve individual strands.
- On a CPU-only computer the status bar shows a red "CUDA is not available" notice at every launch;
  it is informational.
- Tested on Windows 11 with an RTX 5070 (CUDA 13.0) and with the CPU build. The CUDA 12.6 build
  for older NVIDIA GPUs has not been tested on real hardware yet.
